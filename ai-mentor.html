Backpropagation in plain English:
Think of a neural network as a chain of guesses. The network makes a prediction, checks how wrong it was (the "loss"), then walks backwards through each layer calculating: "how much did I contribute to this mistake?" It then nudges each weight slightly in the direction that would have made the guess better. Repeat this millions of times, and the network "learns."

The math in one line:
∂Loss/∂weight = ∂Loss/∂output × ∂output/∂weight

How critical is it for your path?
It's foundational. For your ML Engineer goal, you need conceptual understanding (not every detail) — interviewers at Google and Microsoft regularly ask "explain backprop intuitively." Your current Deep Learning gap is -42 points; mastering backprop covers about 20 of those.
